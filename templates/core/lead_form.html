{% extends "base.html" %} {% comment %} lead_form.html: the desk form — "somebody called, write this down". TEACHING NOTE: it is nearly templates/core/form.html, and deliberately not a {% include %} of it. That shared form renders whatever fields it is handed, including the ones a Rep may not touch, and its page furniture is built around a record that already exists. A lead has no record yet and its whole point is that ANY signed-in user may fill it in — including a Viewer, who cannot open core/form.html at all. Same styling, different rule, so its own template. The layout still comes from core/partials/field.html, so a field looks identical here, on a company and on a deal. `message` spans both columns because it is the one field people write a paragraph into. TEACHING NOTE: the `` below the grid is the Company box's list of names that already exist. It ships EMPTY, and the six `hx-*`/`list` attrs that point the input at it live on the widget in core/forms_leads.py, not here — so this template renders a datalist that has nothing to do with suggestions and the feature is one edit to the form. Filling it is HTMX's job and nobody's else's: as somebody types, `hx-get` asks core/views_leads.py `company_suggestions` for matching names and drops them into this element (`hx-swap="innerHTML"` rewrites its children and leaves the tag alone). The reason it exists at all is a data-quality bug, not a convenience one. `leads._match_or_create_company` matches the company on `name__iexact`, so a tech typing "Lakeside Dentl" creates a SECOND company that reads identically in /companies/ and splits the account's history in half. A suggestion they click hands back the stored spelling exactly, so the two paths cannot disagree. The empty first render is intentional too — an empty datalist is what a person sees before they have typed two characters, and the browser shows nothing for it either way. {% endcomment %} {% block title %}New lead{% endblock %} {% block content %}
