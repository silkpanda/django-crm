TEACHING NOTE — the first column links the DEAL, not the company, because the deal is the thing somebody is being asked to decide about. The Action column holds a different control depending on who is looking, and that is the whole point of the change rather than an inconsistency: - "Pick up" is for everyone who can see the queue. It POSTs to `lead_claim`, and the server decides the owner, so a Rep can only ever claim a lead FOR THEMSELVES. There is no dropdown of other people's names in front of a Rep. - The assign `` of Admins and Reps plus a submit button, one pair per row, and `assign_form` is built ONCE in the view and rendered in every row: the queryset behind it is the permission, and one form means one definition of who may be given a lead (core/forms_leads.LeadAssignForm) rather than one per row that could drift. SECURITY NOTE — both hiding and showing are politeness. The queue is gated on role in the view, and `lead_claim` re-checks the role AND re-checks that the deal is still unowned and still at the Lead stage inside a single conditional UPDATE, so a Rep who hand-writes the claim URL for somebody else's lead gets nothing and an error message rather than the deal. The dropdown disappearing for Reps is a courtesy to the Rep; the 403 in the view is the lock. The Team column answers "who SENT this lead", not "where did the customer first come from", and those are two different questions. It reads `deal.source` — stamped by `create_lead` from whichever team or form took the call — and falls back to `deal.company.source` when that is empty, because every lead filed before `Deal.source` existed has no value there, and showing them what they showed yesterday beats showing a dash.
